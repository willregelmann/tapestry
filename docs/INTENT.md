# Tapestry

Continuity for AI agents: what makes separate sessions feel like one ongoing life.

An agent meets the world in discrete sessions, and each one starts as a stranger. The project you explained yesterday has to be explained again, a decision already made gets reopened, and a correction has to be given twice. Remembering everything isn't the fix. Most of what an agent knows doesn't belong in this conversation: what it knows about one project gets in the way of another, and a user's finances have no place in a chat about their garden. Some of what does belong shares no words with the moment: a question about Thursday should bring the trip to mind. And some of it was true once and isn't now.

So whenever an agent is about to act, Tapestry answers one question: **what should I have in mind right now?** What it knows about each person, project and topic stays in its own place, out of sight until a conversation needs it. One memory brings others to mind, corrections supersede what they correct, what goes unused fades, and between conversations it sleeps on what it learned. Like a person, it keeps what a conversation taught as facts, and lets the words themselves fade.

Continuity adds up to more than convenience. An agent that carries one mind from session to session becomes someone: it has a history, ways of working it has settled into, and a long acquaintance with the people it works with. That identity comes from what it has lived through, never from what it's told it is. And it can learn: to know more, to know how well it knows it, and in time to work better, from what worked and what didn't, from being corrected, and from outcomes it didn't produce itself. It grows better and not merely older.

It's built on one idea: fallibility isn't a flaw, it's a side effect. A memory that can't be wrong can't generalize, abstract or guess, so Tapestry works the way human memory does, and where it departs, it does what people do: it keeps a notebook. Nothing is ever edited, so any belief can be traced to who said it, and when the stakes justify it, the agent simply asks. Each agent's memory is its own, private by default and shared only on purpose.

If a session ever begins as a stranger, or an agent has the wrong things in mind (something stale, something never said, something from another context, or less than it knew when it mattered), Tapestry has failed at the one thing it exists to do.
