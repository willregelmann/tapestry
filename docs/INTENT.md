# Tapestry

Long-term memory for AI agents: facts that come to mind when they matter, and stay out of sight when they don't.

An agent meets the world in discrete sessions, and each one starts as a stranger. The project you explained yesterday has to be explained again, a decision already made gets reopened, and a correction has to be given twice. Remembering everything isn't the fix. A transcript is mostly noise, and plenty of conversations teach nothing worth keeping. What an agent knows about one project gets in the way of another, and a user's finances have no place in a chat about their garden. Some of what does belong shares no words with the moment: a question about Thursday should bring the trip to mind. And some of it was true once and isn't now.

So Tapestry keeps facts, not conversations, and whenever an agent is about to act it answers one question: **what should I have in mind right now?** Most of this happens on its own. After a conversation, the facts it taught are drawn out in the background, and before each turn whatever bears on the moment is put in front of the agent. The agent can also reach in by hand, to look something up, note something, correct it or forget it. What it knows about each person, project and topic stays in its own place, out of sight until a conversation needs it. One memory brings related ones to mind, so the right things arrive even when the words don't match. Corrections supersede what they correct, and what goes unused fades. An agent that carries one mind from session to session builds a history with the people it works with.

It's built on one idea: fallibility isn't a flaw, it's a side effect. A memory that can't be wrong can't generalize, abstract or guess, so Tapestry works the way human memory does, and where it departs, it does what people do: it keeps a notebook. Nothing is ever edited, so any belief can be traced to who said it, and when the stakes justify it, the agent simply asks. Each agent's memory is its own, private by default and shared only on purpose.

If a session ever begins as a stranger, or an agent has the wrong things in mind (something stale, something never said, something from another context, or less than it knew when it mattered), Tapestry has failed at the one thing it exists to do.
